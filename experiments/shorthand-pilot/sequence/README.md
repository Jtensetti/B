# Three changes in the same two model contexts

After observing the first [dispatch batch](../README.md), we continued one participant from each tool setting. The same model contexts kept their original guides and access to their previously accepted dispatch revisions. Each follow-up introduced four new exceptions. The new compartment rule at stage 2 and depot rule at stage 3 each took precedence at their intersection with an earlier rule. Both complete task descriptions and independently tabulated results across all three stages were fixed before dispatching the follow-ups. The follow-up substantially resembles the first task and was selected after seeing its 19-token output difference.

| Initial dispatch exceptions | Ordinary directives | Compact rows | 46 / 27 | 397 / 430 | 397 / 430 |

Every submitted revision in the two contexts passed 44/44 observations plus interface checks on its first submission. The two solvers each received exactly one product brief per follow-up. Neither asked for the source to be displayed again or received another syntax guide. Their brief and public-result text consumed 66 and 8 tokens respectively at each follow-up. The count uses `o200k_base` and measures the same text categories as the initial experiment. The transition prompts, host/tool envelopes, internal reasoning, assistant status messages, cache effects and billing were outside the measurement. No real-user evaluation was collected.

The solver with only ordinary B voluntarily sent the entire 203-token program at stage 2. It had retained access to the ordinary directive workflow. The observed 163-token difference therefore measures two solvers' complete choices on this task sequence. We also expanded the compact rows with B and counted the corresponding ordinary directive payloads: 46, 45 and 46 tokens. The compact rows used 27, 26 and 27. With equivalent choices, three batches save 57 generated tokens, offsetting the 52 extra tokens of the single optional guide. This controlled reconstruction gives 636 counted tokens in ordinary B against the observed 631 with shorthand. Of the 163-token observed gap, 158 come from the one choice to regenerate a whole program. The reconstructed ordinary stage-2 payload was checked against the same 44 observations. This comparison does not establish a general traffic mix, causal effect on model choices, or cost reduction in deployment.

Each new request was evaluated against the participant's own previous accepted source. The follow-up oracle was frozen at SHA-256 `e1fc341494736b78b8bdfdce2fd2ac2a71273c612be8c01026b76af90ca531a0` for [sequence.py](sequence.py), and the packet recorder at `632cece674c1101ff52c2d1d9669bed856ff16fa7efb2607ffa84cae51691112` for [round.py](round.py). Reapplying only the new changes to the initial unmodified program passes the public observations at both stages. The independent scores drop to 38/44 at stage 2 and 32/44 at stage 3. This control shows that the broader oracle captures lost earlier behavior.
